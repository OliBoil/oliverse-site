After yesterday’s on-site installation in Barcelona, today I was back in the workshop and started by reviewing the tasks that were still pending.

One of the first jobs was preparing a new OLED laptop for its owner. We completed the Windows 11 setup with a local account, installed the latest updates and added essential applications such as Google Chrome, VLC, WinRAR and a PDF reader. The OLED display looked impressive, but the most important part was making sure the computer was fully updated and ready to use.

We also diagnosed an older desktop computer that was not working correctly. After opening it, we found a loose screw making contact near the motherboard’s CMOS battery. We then discovered that the fan inside the power supply was not working, which was causing the unit to overheat severely. Because the power supply had failed and had become dangerously hot, replacing it was essential. After installing a new one, the system appeared to work normally again.

Another interesting task was testing a recovery process using an Acronis-based cloud backup system. We created a bootable USB drive from the recovery ISO and tried to restore a backup on a spare laptop. The process produced several errors, so we could not complete the restoration and will need to continue investigating it. Even so, it helped me understand how cloud backups can be recovered on another device—and why a backup is only truly useful when its restoration process has been tested.

I also helped assemble a new desktop computer from its individual components. We installed the power supply and cooling system, organised the cabling, installed the operating system and prepared the machine for use.

Finally, we worked on a laptop whose drive was protected with BitLocker but whose recovery key was unavailable. Following the customer’s request, we erased the drive, reinstalled Windows 11, applied the updates and installed the usual essential software.

It was a fairly normal workshop day, but it included a useful mix of hardware diagnosis, system deployment, backup recovery and PC assembly. Every task added something different to the learning experience.
